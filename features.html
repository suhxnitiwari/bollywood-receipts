The men who said it happened to them

Bollywood's casting couch, from the side that gets talked about least.

Several male actors have said they were propositioned while trying to break in. They tell these stories far less often than actresses do, and they almost never name anyone. We don't either: when they didn't name the person, there's no name here, and nothing on the map points at one.

Ranveer Singh

Before Band Baaja Baaraat, carrying his portfolio, he was invited to a man's home in Andheri for what he thought was a work meeting. He describes a "highly sleazy gentleman" who wanted him to be open to more than auditions, and says he walked out. In his words, the casting couch is very real.

Ayushmann Khurrana

Before Vicky Donor, he says a casting director offered him a lead role in return for a sexual favour. He said he was straight and refused. His conclusion: in the end, talent is what lasts.

Rajeev Khandelwal

Early in his career, he says a well-known director made a work offer that turned out to be about something else, and he turned it down. He has told the story more than once, and he has never named the director.

Ravi Kishan

On Aap Ki Adalat: "Yeah, it happened." In his account it was a woman who later became powerful in the industry, and he won't say who. It's a reminder that the thing to look at is who controls the work, not anyone's gender.

The pattern

All four stories happened when the men had no power at all: struggling Ranveer, Ayushmann before his debut, Rajeev before films, young Ravi. Someone controlled access to work and tried to trade it for sex. Each of them says he refused, and each made it anyway.

What we label it

Casting couch means sex offered or demanded in exchange for work. Harassment, assault allegations and unwanted advances are different things, and get their own labels. Someone hitting on an actor isn't a casting couch.

What we won't do

Solve the blind items. "A prominent director" stays a prominent director. And refusing an unwanted advance says nothing about gay men in general: the abuse is the trade of work for sex.

Kangana kept trying to make Taapsee and Alia rivals. Taapsee never took the bait

The outsider, the insider, and the woman grading them both.

On paper, and are the perfect opposites: South films and a software job on one side, a Bhatt and a Karan Johar launch on the other. The feud everyone expects is missing. The one actually on record is with the woman who kept comparing them.

    Pink against Udta Punjab and Dear Zindagi. Manmarziyaan against Raazi. At the 2020 Filmfares, Alia wins Best Actress for Gully Boy, and Taapsee shares Critics' Best Actress with Bhumi for Saand Ki Aankh. The film that won and the critics' pick, side by side.

  2. Jul 2020

    On Arnab Goswami's show she calls Taapsee and Swara Bhasker "B-grade actresses" and "needy outsiders," and asks why, if they're "better looking than Alia Bhatt and Ananya" and "better actresses," they aren't A-list. Alia hadn't said a word. She was just the yardstick.

  3. Jul 2020

    Taapsee doesn't take it

    She doesn't reply that she's better than Alia. She replies to Kangana: "Don't be bitter, be better." Swara adds up her report card, "needy outsider, B-grade actress… better looking and better actor than Alia Bhatt and Ananya," and decides: "Net net, I think this was a compliment!"

  4. 2019

    Where the feud started

    Taapsee praised the Judgementall Hai Kya trailer without naming Kangana, and Rangoli Chandel called her a "sasti copy" of her sister. Taapsee's reply was a joke: Kangana is the highest-paid actress, so a copy of her would be the cheaper version.

The problem, she says, is opportunity that keeps coming however someone performs. Alia has said much the same: she had a head start, and the work is how she justifies it.

And the strangest part: in 2026 Taapsee said she has never met Kangana, not before the feud and not since. In August Kangana took Taapsee's remark about their different upbringings as a dig at her parents, and brought back "sasti copy." Taapsee posted "Hence, proved," then asked reporters why they won't let it end. Seven years of feud between two women who have never been in the same room.

Never established

Two wives, one law

Dharmendra, Mahesh Bhatt, and the loophole the Supreme Court closed in 1995.

Two of Bollywood's best-known families rest on a second marriage made while the first was still going. In both, the story goes that the husband converted to Islam to get around Hindu law's one-spouse rule. One of those stories is on the record. The other has been denied for twenty years.

Dharmendra

Married in 1954, four children, never divorced. Married around 1979–80. In 2004, rivals put a nikahnama before a court recording "Dilawar Khan" marrying "Ayesha Bi." It doesn't use the name Dharmendra (one report has it reading "Dilawar Khan Kewal Krishn", and his father was Kewal Krishan), and it was never verified. He called the claim "totally incorrect," and Hema said there was "no truth" in it. Disputed. Read the full case file →

Mahesh Bhatt

Married , later Kiran, in 1970, with children Pooja and Rahul. Widely reported to have converted before marrying in a private nikah in 1986, so he wouldn't have to divorce Kiran. Soni's name was Sakina. Pooja has discussed it openly, and says Soni told her "I too feel so guilty." Reported, and not denied.

Then the law caught up

  1. 1955

    One spouse

    The Hindu Marriage Act makes a second marriage void while the first spouse is alive, and bigamy a crime. Muslim personal law allows up to four wives. That gap is the loophole.

  2. 1995

    Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India

    The Supreme Court closes it. Converting to Islam doesn't dissolve a Hindu marriage, so a second marriage made after converting is void, and the husband can be prosecuted for bigamy. The ruling came after both of these marriages.

  3. Since

    And no, there's no prenup

    India has no law making prenuptial agreements binding the way they are in the US. Courts can treat one as an ordinary contract, but it can't override marriage law on maintenance or custody. Goa is the exception: its civil code has its own marital property rules and allows a pre-marriage property contract.

Not the same story

Sharmila Tagore became Ayesha Sultana when she married Mansoor Ali Khan Pataudi, and A. R. Rahman converted as a young man, years before he married. Neither involved a second marriage, and neither had anything to do with a loophole. And despite what social media says, Swara Bhasker didn't convert: she married Fahad Ahmad in 2023 under the Special Marriage Act, which exists so that neither partner has to.

What we don't say

That Dharmendra converted. That Sunny and Bobby are estranged from Hema. In 2026 she said the family is "very close to each other," with "no negativity at all."

A century of the sexy heroine

How Bollywood kept redefining a "respectable woman" while selling her sexuality

Bollywood didn't just get steadily bolder. It kissed in 1933, went prudish after independence, invented the cabaret and the wet sari to get around the censors, and then let the heroine take it all back.

THE ARC

1930s: kissing→1950s–60s: desire coded into dance and clothes→1970s–80s: heroine and vamp merge→1990s: the heroine owns it→2000s: kissing returns

THE DANCER LINEAGE

Azurie→Cuckoo→Helen→Bindu, Aruna Irani→Zeenat, Parveen→Madhuri→Raveena, Urmila→Malaika

How the films got away with it

Readings from the University of Iowa Indian Cinema archive

Code it, don't show it

Rain, waterfalls, wet saris, flowers, bathing, dream songs. Censorship didn't remove sex from Hindi film; it built a whole visual vocabulary for it.

The "natural" girl

City man meets mountain or village girl: pure, traditional, and somehow always bathing or dancing in the rain. Her innocence becomes the excuse for the camera's gaze. From Kashmir Ki Kali (1964) to Taal (1999).

The premarital-sex rule

Old films allowed it on two conditions: the couple treats it as a marriage in all but name, and she gets pregnant. Aradhana (1969) is the template.

The courtesan loophole

The tawaif could sing, dance and love outside marriage because she was never fully respectable. Pakeezah, Umrao Jaan. In Guide (1965), Rosie leaves her husband and becomes a celebrated dancer, mirroring how India rebranded courtesan dance as respectable classical art.

Clothes as morals

Sari means home and virtue; Western dress means city, money, desire, danger. Yet the films give the "dangerous" woman the best outfits, songs and nightclubs.

Raj Kapoor's contradiction

Films about poverty, faith and the nation, shot by a camera with what the archive calls a "voyeuristic preoccupation with female anatomy." Satyam Shivam Sundaram preaches that looks don't matter while eroticizing Zeenat, and splits her into rejected wife by day and desired stranger by night.

Based on close readings of 100+ films by Philip Lutgendorf, with Corey Creekmur, at the University of Iowa Indian Cinema project. It's film criticism, not a data study.

The timeline

  1. 1933

    Devika Rani kisses on screen

    In Karma, she and her real-life husband Himanshu Rai share a series of kisses. Often called a four-minute kiss; careful accounts say it was shorter. Either way, Indian cinema kissed before it stopped.

  2. 1935–47

    Madam Azurie, the first star dancer

    Doing standalone dance numbers in Hindi films decades before anyone said "item song." She trained Cuckoo.

  3. 1940s–50s

    Cuckoo Moray, the "Rubber Girl"

    Made the cabaret dance a fixture of commercial Hindi film, and helped a young Helen get into movies.

  4. 1951

    Nargis in a swimsuit

    Raj Kapoor's Awaara starts a line of boundary-pushing images he kept extending for 35 years: Nargis, then Dimple, then Zeenat, then Mandakini.

  5. 1950s

    , flirt in a strapless dress

    Strapless gowns, capris, trousers and a very knowing look at the camera. Innocence and sensuality in one face.

  6. 1950s–70s

    , the cabaret queen

    "Mera Naam Chin Chin Chu," "Piya Tu Ab To Aaja," "Mehbooba Mehbooba." The era split women in two: the sari-clad heroine, and the vamp who could drink, smoke, dance and want things. Helen ruled the second.

  7. 1960s–70s

    Bindu, Aruna Irani and Mumtaz

    Bindu's "Mera Naam Shabnam" (Kati Patang, 1971) played straight at the men watching. Mumtaz brought some of that heat into the actual heroine.

  8. 1970s

    Zeenat Aman and Parveen Babi break the system

    The Westernized, sexually confident woman becomes the heroine the hero loves, not the vamp next to her. Zeenat's Satyam Shivam Sundaram (1978) pushed costume and kissing further than mainstream film had gone.

  9. 1973–85

    , Bobby to Saagar

    Bobby's bikini shaped youth fashion, though she was still a teenager, cast at 14, which reads very differently now. As an adult she shared a landmark kiss with Rishi Kapoor in Saagar (1985).

  10. 1985

    Mandakini's waterfall

    Ram Teri Ganga Maili's thin white sari under a waterfall: technically clothed, and everyone understood. Raj Kapoor's most notorious test of the censors.

  11. 1988–92

    Madhuri Dixit

    A kiss with Vinod Khanna in Dayavan, then "Dhak Dhak Karne Laga." Sensual dance now belongs to the country's biggest heroine.

  12. 1994–95

    and Urmila

    "Tip Tip Barsa Paani" in a yellow sari in the rain, then Urmila's crop tops and beachwear in Rangeela, played as a modern young woman, not a dangerous one. The heroine–vamp split is dead.

  13. 1996

    and Aamir

    The Raja Hindustani kiss, in a giant mainstream blockbuster. Kissing belongs to the romantic heroine again.

  14. 1996–98

    Shabana Azmi and Nandita Das in Fire

    Two women, desire and intimacy inside unhappy marriages. Not a sexier scene but a different question: female desire that doesn't revolve around a man.
