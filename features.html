Kareena Kapoor, before the media training

Before every star learned to say "she's amazing, I love her work," Bebo was naming names

Raj Kapoor's granddaughter arrived in 2000 already acting like Bollywood belonged to her. Where 's quotes were about taste, young 's were simpler: I'm the star. She said who couldn't act, whose accent was fake and what wasn't sexy, and she survived long enough to build the résumé that makes it funny. Every quote here is on the record.

The record

  1. 1999–2000

    The film she didn't make

    Cast opposite in Kaho Naa… Pyaar Hai, she shot a song, then left. Kareena says she chose Refugee; Ameesha has said Rakesh Roshan asked her to go. Ameesha took the part and became a star overnight. The rivalry was born on day one.

  2. 2001

    Ajnabee

    A film with newcomer Bipasha Basu, and Bollywood's most retold catfight. The "kaali billi" insult and the slap are still only reported. Our is stamped alleged.

  3. c. 2005

    The accent

    On Koffee with Karan, about her Aitraaz co-star: "Where did she get that accent from?" Priyanka, on a later episode: "The same place her boyfriend gets it from." The boyfriend was UK-schooled .

  4. 2007

    "Expressionless"

    Koffee again: she didn't want to work with John Abraham, Bipasha's boyfriend, because he was expressionless. Later that season Bipasha answered for him: Kareena is "someone with too many expressions." John said no comment.

  5. 2007–08

    The breakup

    She and split around Jab We Met, the film that made Geet immortal. Asked if he'd work with her again, he said that if his director asked him to romance a cow or a buffalo, he'd do it. "It's my job."

  6. 2011

    Ra.One

    Her terms for a superhero film with , at the wrap party: "I don't want to do any action-waction… Just make me look glamorous." He fell off buildings. She did the songs.

  7. 2012

    Size zero

    "Being fat is not sexy! Anyone who says that is talking crap. Voluptuous is sexy, but fat is out. Any woman who says she doesn't want to be thin is talking nonsense. It's every girl's dream." Vidya had just gained weight for The Dirty Picture. When Kareena's Heroine drew comparisons, Vidya: "They could make a Heroine but no one will be able to make The Dirty Picture."

  8. May 2018

    "I wouldn't say I am a feminist"

    At the Veere Di Wedding music launch: "I believe in equality. I wouldn't say I am a feminist." Her producer , Sonam's sister, corrected her on the spot: that's "the actual definition of a feminist if you look up in the dictionary."

  9. 2018

    Truce

    Kareena and Priyanka share the Koffee couch in season 6 and laugh about the old war.

  10. 2020

    "Audience make us stars"

    On nepotism: "Don't go to watch movies if you don't want them to be stars," and, to Barkha Dutt, "Aap jaa re ho na film dekhne? Mat jao." Two years later the clip was recycled to push a boycott of Laal Singh Chaddha, a film she wasn't talking about.

Why it's funny now

She outlasted all of it. Chameli, Omkara, Jab We Met, Talaash, Udta Punjab (with Shahid, by then a non-event) and Jaane Jaan. The ego arrived first; the filmography caught up. She made peace with Priyanka on camera, and the old rivals barely register now.

Quotes we couldn't trace

Repeated for years, but we found no original source: that no actress in 15 years had played a role as deep as Main Prem Ki Diwani Hoon, that scripts put her to sleep, the laptop that was "a green one," calling Sonakshi a "housewife," and the line about Ameesha's skin.

Sonam Kapoor, said out loud

Taste, pedigree and total candour: how one star kept saying the part Bollywood usually leaves unsaid

Bollywood runs on hierarchy: age, looks, family, fashion, PR. Most stars wrap it in press-release language. kept saying it plainly, for over a decade. Sometimes that made her the most entertaining person in the room. Sometimes it made her sound like the reason people resent star kids. Everything below is something she actually said.

THE PATTERN

age→looks→fashion→PR→pedigree→privilege

The record

  1. 2007

    The launch

    Anil Kapoor's daughter debuts opposite Rishi Kapoor's son in Bhansali's Saawariya. Both had been assistant directors on Black. The film flops; her wardrobe doesn't. She has said that, at the risk of sounding immodest, she started trends.

  2. 2009

    "Aunty"

    Signed by L'Oréal, where was already the brand's big Indian face, Sonam called her an aunty from another generation. Her explanation: "Ash has worked with my dad so I have to call her aunty na." Aishwarya was 36. Sonam later said she was misquoted.

  3. Nov 2010

    Allies, briefly

    On Koffee with Karan, Sonam and spend an episode roasting Ranbir, Deepika's ex. Ranbir's father Rishi Kapoor made it known he was offended.

  4. 2012

    A bouquet for Katrina

    Asked by Anupama Chopra at Cannes what she'd give : "a bouquet of flowers… You need a certain kind of commitment and a certain kind of shamelessness to do certain things." She said she'd need to develop that herself first.

  5. 2013–14

    Ally to rival

    Back on Koffee, now without Deepika. Advice for her? "Create your own style." Then, praising Katrina: "I'd rather have that than someone who is like 'I want to be on the cover of Vogue every three months'." What does Deepika have that she doesn't? An over-enthusiastic PR team. Good girl gone bad? Deepika.

  6. Sep 2014

    The thaw

    At the Finding Fanny screening, Deepika welcomes her, they pose together and praise each other.

  7. 2016

    Neerja

    The answer to "fashion, not acting." No superstar hero, no ensemble: a film built on her performance as the flight attendant who died saving passengers in 1986. It wins Best Hindi Film at the National Awards, and Sonam a Special Mention.

  8. 2017

    The dictionary

    As the nepotism fight between and spreads, Sonam explains the word: getting a job through a relative or friend that you don't deserve. People, she said, were using it without looking it up in a dictionary or on Wikipedia. Her own surname did the rest of the commentary.

  9. 2018

    Kangana

    Asked about Kangana's #MeToo account: "sometimes it's hard to take her seriously," alongside praise for her spunk. Kangana's reply: Sonam "isn't known to be a great actress." Sonam said she'd been taken out of context.

  10. Jun 2020

    "My karma"

    Facing abuse in the insider-outsider fury after Sushant Singh Rajput's death, on Father's Day: "yes I'm my fathers daughter and yes I am here because of him and yes I'm privileged. That's not an insult… And it is my karma where I'm born and to whom I'm born." The first half was the most candid thing a star kid had said. The second half suggested birth is deserved.

What's fair

She acknowledged privilege when few star kids would. She patched things up with Deepika within a year. Neerja earned her the praise she'd said she was denied. And the 2020 post came while she was being sent real abuse.

Quotes we couldn't trace

These circulate as Sonam lines but we found no original interview, so they're not here: "nobody else out there can do what I do," "a fashion diva who can't act," Kangana's "crown of the ugliest person" comeback, and the line about Indian men and fashion.

A century of the sexy heroine

How Bollywood kept redefining a "respectable woman" while selling her sexuality

Bollywood didn't just get steadily bolder. It kissed in 1933, went prudish after independence, invented the cabaret and the wet sari to get around the censors, and then let the heroine take it all back.

THE ARC

1930s: kissing→1950s–60s: desire coded into dance and clothes→1970s–80s: heroine and vamp merge→1990s: the heroine owns it→2000s: kissing returns

THE DANCER LINEAGE

Azurie→Cuckoo→Helen→Bindu, Aruna Irani→Zeenat, Parveen→Madhuri→Raveena, Urmila→Malaika

How the films got away with it

Readings from the University of Iowa Indian Cinema archive

Code it, don't show it

Rain, waterfalls, wet saris, flowers, bathing, dream songs. Censorship didn't remove sex from Hindi film; it built a whole visual vocabulary for it.

The "natural" girl

City man meets mountain or village girl: pure, traditional, and somehow always bathing or dancing in the rain. Her innocence becomes the excuse for the camera's gaze. From Kashmir Ki Kali (1964) to Taal (1999).

The premarital-sex rule

Old films allowed it on two conditions: the couple treats it as a marriage in all but name, and she gets pregnant. Aradhana (1969) is the template.

The courtesan loophole

The tawaif could sing, dance and love outside marriage because she was never fully respectable. Pakeezah, Umrao Jaan. In Guide (1965), Rosie leaves her husband and becomes a celebrated dancer, mirroring how India rebranded courtesan dance as respectable classical art.

Clothes as morals

Sari means home and virtue; Western dress means city, money, desire, danger. Yet the films give the "dangerous" woman the best outfits, songs and nightclubs.

Raj Kapoor's contradiction

Films about poverty, faith and the nation, shot by a camera with what the archive calls a "voyeuristic preoccupation with female anatomy." Satyam Shivam Sundaram preaches that looks don't matter while eroticizing Zeenat, and splits her into rejected wife by day and desired stranger by night.

Based on close readings of 100+ films by Philip Lutgendorf, with Corey Creekmur, at the University of Iowa Indian Cinema project. It's film criticism, not a data study.

The timeline

  1. 1933

    Devika Rani kisses on screen

    In Karma, she and her real-life husband Himanshu Rai share a series of kisses. Often called a four-minute kiss; careful accounts say it was shorter. Either way, Indian cinema kissed before it stopped.

  2. 1935–47

    Madam Azurie, the first star dancer

    Doing standalone dance numbers in Hindi films decades before anyone said "item song." She trained Cuckoo.

  3. 1940s–50s

    Cuckoo Moray, the "Rubber Girl"

    Made the cabaret dance a fixture of commercial Hindi film, and helped a young Helen get into movies.

  4. 1951

    Nargis in a swimsuit

    Raj Kapoor's Awaara starts a line of boundary-pushing images he kept extending for 35 years: Nargis, then Dimple, then Zeenat, then Mandakini.

  5. 1950s

    , flirt in a strapless dress

    Strapless gowns, capris, trousers and a very knowing look at the camera. Innocence and sensuality in one face.

  6. 1950s–70s

    , the cabaret queen

    "Mera Naam Chin Chin Chu," "Piya Tu Ab To Aaja," "Mehbooba Mehbooba." The era split women in two: the sari-clad heroine, and the vamp who could drink, smoke, dance and want things. Helen ruled the second.

  7. 1960s–70s

    Bindu, Aruna Irani and Mumtaz

    Bindu's "Mera Naam Shabnam" (Kati Patang, 1971) played straight at the men watching. Mumtaz brought some of that heat into the actual heroine.

  8. 1970s

    Zeenat Aman and Parveen Babi break the system

    The Westernized, sexually confident woman becomes the heroine the hero loves, not the vamp next to her. Zeenat's Satyam Shivam Sundaram (1978) pushed costume and kissing further than mainstream film had gone.

  9. 1973–85

    , Bobby to Saagar

    Bobby's bikini shaped youth fashion, though she was 16 during the shoot, which reads very differently now. As an adult she shared a landmark kiss with Rishi Kapoor in Saagar (1985).

  10. 1985

    Mandakini's waterfall

    Ram Teri Ganga Maili's thin white sari under a waterfall: technically clothed, and everyone understood. Raj Kapoor's most notorious test of the censors.

  11. 1988–92

    Madhuri Dixit

    A kiss with Vinod Khanna in Dayavan, then "Dhak Dhak Karne Laga." Sensual dance now belongs to the country's biggest heroine.

  12. 1994–95

    and Urmila

    "Tip Tip Barsa Paani" in a yellow sari in the rain, then Urmila's crop tops and beachwear in Rangeela, played as a modern young woman, not a dangerous one. The heroine–vamp split is dead.

  13. 1996

    and Aamir

    The Raja Hindustani kiss, in a giant mainstream blockbuster. Kissing belongs to the romantic heroine again.

  14. 1996–98

    Shabana Azmi and Nandita Das in Fire

    Two women, desire and intimacy inside unhappy marriages. Not a sexier scene but a different question: female desire that doesn't revolve around a man.

The theory · Feature

Alia's inheritance board

The "Alia inherits other actresses' lanes" theory, checked line by line. There's a real pattern: she idolized Kareena, trained with Katrina before marrying Katrina's ex, and followed Deepika into Bhansali, L'Oréal and Levi's. "She copies everything" doesn't hold up. Gucci, her kids' brand Ed-a-Mamma, and her own production company Eternal Sunshine (Darlings) are hers alone.
